In the classroom — High and low, long and short

High and low, long and short

Level 1 · Lesson 2 · high and low, long and short

Teacher’s guide. © 2026 Music Motion.

For: ages 8 to 12, the whole class. Length: 50 minutes.

Main objective: by the end of the lesson, each pair reads a drawing of three or four lines on the board (high or low, short or long) and plays it on the keyboard; the class gives a thumbs-up if it sounded like the drawing.

Secondary objectives: (1) show with the body whether a sound is high or low; (2) count with the pulse how many clicks a long sound lasts.

What you need: a computer with speakers, the board and, if available, a projector. It helps to have done Lesson 1. No music knowledge needed: the keyboard does the work; you guide.

Before the lesson (10 minutes, at home): practise the keyboard. In class your attention needs to be on the children, not on the buttons. Repeat these steps until you can do them without looking at the guide:

  • Open the Cosmic Keyboard. Press Z and then U: the first is low and the second is high.
  • Play Z X C V B N M, press ← twice and play it again: the whole staircase goes down. Press → twice to go back to the middle.
  • Open the metronome with pulse, set about 60 BPM with tap, press start and switch on click, as in Lesson 1.
  • With the metronome running, play Z and let go straight away (one click); then hold it down for four clicks. The sound lasts as long as the key is held down.

Before we start: two words from music

High and low

In everyday life, “up” and “down” are often used for volume: “turn the music down”. Not in music: high and low describe the pitch of a sound. A high sound is thin and bright, like birdsong or a whistle. A low sound is thick and deep, like thunder, a big drum or the double bass in an orchestra.

Volume is something else: a sound can be high and soft, or low and loud. In this lesson we do not change the volume.

Long and short

A sound can also last a short or a long time. To measure how long it lasts we use the pulse from Lesson 1: a short sound lasts one click of the metronome (“ta”); a long sound lasts several clicks (“taaaa”). On the keyboard, the sound lasts as long as the key is held down.

For the metronome: pulse, a slow tempo with tap (about 60 BPM), start and click, as in Lesson 1.

The lesson, step by step

The lesson follows five stages: the goal, what they already know, the new material, the application and the review of the goal. In each activity, you do it first and the class follows you.

  1. 3 min

    The goal

    Tell the class, in these words or similar ones:

    “Today we are going to learn to draw a sound and to play a drawing. At the end, every pair will play on the keyboard what we draw on the board.”

    Write the goal on the board: I can draw a sound and play a drawing.

  2. 5 min

    What they already know: the staircase and the pulse

    With the metronome on slow, play Z X C V B N M, one key per click. The class sings each note on an “ah” and keeps the pulse with their feet, as in Lesson 1.

    “Does the staircase go up or down? Where does the lowest note start?”

  3. 17 min

    New material: high and low, long and short

    1. The lift (5 min). Play a low note (Z) and then a high one (U, on the top row). Show it yourself first: crouching down for the low note, standing with arms up for the high one. Then single notes at random: the class goes up or down according to what they hear. Harder: play two notes in a row; does the second go up or down? The class shows it with the hand.
    2. The singing glasses (2 min). Before the lesson, fill four identical glasses: one full, one half full, one a third full and one empty. Tap them with a spoon, in no particular order, and the class does the lift: crouching if the sound is low, standing if it is high. Then ask: “Which glass sounds lowest?” It is the same picture as in the pupil’s workbook, My Little Music Grammar · Level 1, chapter 2.
    3. The same song, up and down (3 min). Play the staircase on the bottom row. Press ← twice and play it again: it sounds much lower. Press → four times (two to go back to the middle and two more) and play it again: now it is very high. It is the same melody, at a different height.
    4. Long and short (7 min). With the metronome on slow, play Z and let go straight away: it is a short sound, one click. The class claps once. Then hold Z down for four clicks: it is a long sound. The class opens their arms slowly while it sounds and closes them when it stops. Mix short and long sounds without warning; the class counts aloud how many clicks each long sound lasts: two, three or four.

    “What animal would this sound be? A mouse or an elephant? Which one sounds like a giant and which like a fairy?”

  4. 20 min

    Application: drawing the sound and playing the drawing

    Drawing the sound (10 min). On the board, draw a line halfway up: it is the “middle floor”. A high sound is drawn above the line, a low one below it. A short sound is a short dash; a long one, a long dash.

    First you play three or four sounds and a pupil draws on the board what they hear; the class says whether the drawing is right. Repeat with other pupils.

    Playing the drawing (10 min). Now the other way round. Show it yourself first with this example, with the metronome running: three short sounds going up and one long sound down low.

    1. A pupil draws three or four dashes on the board.
    2. A pair comes up to the computer: one points to each dash and the other plays it (bottom row for low, top row for high; short is one click, long is several clicks).
    3. The class gives a thumbs-up if it sounded like the drawing. If not, the pair tries again.
    4. The pairs take turns. While one pair plays, the rest show with their bodies what they hear: up or down, arms closed or open.

    This drawing is the grandparent of the score: in the next lesson, the dashes become notes on five lines. That is also how the monks of the Middle Ages began: before the stave, they drew the melody with little signs that went up and down above the words, called neumes.

  5. 5 min

    Reviewing the goal

    Go back to the sentence on the board. Make one last drawing of four dashes; the pairs who have not had a turn yet play it, and the class gives a thumbs-up if it matches.

    “How did you know whether a dash went up or down? And whether it was long or short?”

    Listen to the answers: listening for whether the sound is thin or thick, looking at the row on the keyboard, counting the metronome’s clicks.

With the Melody Machine: up, down, long and short

To end the lesson, or to start the next one. Open the Melody Machine, lower the Tempo to about 80 and press SPIN.

  1. The lift. Press PLAY: the class shows with the hand whether the melody goes up or down, as in today’s lift.
  2. Long and short. PLAY again: every time a long note sounds, the class opens their arms slowly; for the short ones, a clap.
  3. The drawing in the machine. Show the screen: each note is higher or lower on five lines, like the dashes on the board. In the next lesson they will learn to read them.
  4. Up or down? The same machine has the game Up or down?: two notes sound and the class shows with the hand whether the second goes up or down. There are ten rounds, with the notes closer together each time. The pupils also play it in their workbook (chapter 2).

With HOLD the class keeps the bar they like best and carries on spinning.

If they find it hard:
  • They mix up high with loud: sing the same note yourself, softly and then loudly: it is the same pitch. Then play Z and U: that really is low and high. Remind them: “high and low means thin or thick, not more or less noise”.
  • They cannot tell two close notes apart: use notes far apart, Z and U, and bring them closer little by little. Ask them to close their eyes to listen.
  • They let go of the key too early: count the clicks aloud with them (“one, two, three, four”) and lift your own hand at the end.
  • The drawing does not match what they play: play it dash by dash, slowly; after each sound the class says whether it was up or down, short or long.

The bottom row is the lowest and the top row the highest; all three have the same staircase, from C to B. The ← and → arrow keys change the octave: the whole staircase goes down or up.

Note names: C = Do · D = Re · E = Mi · F = Fa · G = Sol · A = La · B = Si. Some materials use the syllables: both name the same note.

How to tell it worked: at least three out of four pairs play the drawing on the board with the right pitch and the right length, and the class confirms it with a thumbs-up. In addition, most pupils show with their body whether a sound is high or low and count the clicks of a long sound correctly.

Up and down, long and short at home

For parents · 10 minutes a week · no music knowledge needed

Today in class we learned that a sound can be high or low, and long or short. This week, play together:

  1. Open the keyboard on the computer: musicmotionsuite.com/apps/mmk-demo.html
  2. The lift: one person plays a key on the bottom row (low) or the top row (high); the other crouches down or stretches up. Then swap.
  3. Long or short: one person holds a key down for a while or lets go straight away; the other opens their arms for as long as the sound lasts.
  4. Sound hunters: find around the house a high sound (a doorbell, a bird), a low one (the washing machine, a passing lorry), a long one and a short one. Draw them as in class: up or down, a short or a long dash.
  5. The Melody Machine: open musicmotionsuite.com/suite/tragamonedas.html and press SPIN and PLAY. Does the melody go up or down? Where is there a long note? With PRINT you can print the melody on a sheet of paper. No registration needed.
  6. The puppy’s walk: open musicmotionsuite.com/suite/tragamonedas.html#paseo. Canelo walks to the pulse of the melody: tap TAP HERE (or the space bar) on every step. Don’t let him trip! First the child, then the adult.
  7. The singing glasses: fill several identical glasses with different amounts of water and tap them gently with a spoon. Which one sounds higher: the one with more water or the one with less?
