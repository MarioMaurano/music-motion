In the classroom — Chords

Chords

Level 2 · Lesson 10 · Part 1 · three notes at once, major and minor, the degrees · Antiquity: the kithara of the Seikilos song with chords

Teacher’s guide. © 2026 Music Motion.

For: ages 9 to 14, the whole class and then in groups. Length: 50 minutes.

Main objective: by the end of the lesson, each group plays on the Keyboard the nine chords of the Seikilos kithara, one per bar, together with the melody; the class says whether each chord is major or minor.

Secondary objectives: (1) build a chord on any note by “taking one, skipping one”; (2) say which degrees of the key give major chords (I, IV, V) and which give minor ones (ii, iii, vi).

What you need: a computer with speakers and internet, the Cosmic Keyboard, the Seikilos song in the Score in its two versions (with the drone from Lesson 7 and with the chords from this lesson), the board and, if possible, a projector. It helps to have done Lessons 6, 7 and 8.

Before the lesson (10 minutes, at home): practise.

  • On the Keyboard, play Z C B together: that is the chord of C major. Then X V N: D minor. Listen to how the character changes.
  • Listen to the Seikilos song with chords while looking at the lower stave: one chord per bar.
  • Play the chords with the melody playing in the other tab: B M S G major · C B M E minor · X V N D minor · V N A F major.

Before we start: three notes at once

Take one, skip one

A chord is three notes sounding together. You build it on the staircase, skipping one each time: C (skip D) E (skip F) G. The bottom note, the root, gives the chord its name: it is the chord of C. The three notes make two thirds, one on top of the other (Lesson 6), and on the stave they are stacked up: all three on lines or all three in spaces.

From the staircase we take one note and skip the next.

Together, they are the chord of C.

Major and minor

The first third, the bottom one, decides. If it is a major third, two tones (C–E), the chord is major: it sounds bright and steady. If it is a minor third, a tone and a diatonic semitone (D–F), the chord is minor: it sounds darker, more tender or sadder. The difference lies in a single note, and the ear feels it straight away.

C–E, two tones: major.

D–F, a tone and a diatonic semitone: minor.

On the Tonal staircase, the major third is two tones of 9/8: 81/64. The minor third is a tone and a diatonic semitone, 9/8 × 256/243: 32/27.

The degrees and their chords

In Lesson 8 the staircase of the key had seven steps, the degrees, with Roman numerals. A chord can be built on each degree, always taking one and skipping one, with the notes of the key. In a major key you get three major chords, on degrees I, IV and V, and three minor ones, on degrees ii, iii and vi; vii is diminished (two minor thirds). In C major: C, F and G are major; D, E and A are minor.

On the Keyboard in Tonal, the bottom row is the degrees: Z C B is always the chord of I, in whatever key you choose with KEY and MODE. The sheet at the end shows it step by step.

The time machine: the Seikilos kithara with chords

Greece, 1st century. In Lesson 7 the class played the drone of the Seikilos kithara: one long, low note per bar. Now a chord is built on each note of the drone, taking one and skipping one. That gives four different chords, two major and two minor:

G, G, E minor, D minor, G, F, G, D minor, G: the root of each chord is the drone note.

The Greeks did not use chords like ours: the kithara played the drone and a few single notes, and three-note chords came to European music many centuries later. Here we dress the oldest melody the way a musician would today, to learn how a harmony is built. In the Score, the Seikilos song with chords has the aulos melody at the top and the chords underneath.

The lesson, step by step

The lesson follows five stages: the goal, what they already know, the new material, the application and the review of the goal. In each activity, you do it first and the class follows you.

  1. 3 min

    The goal

    Tell the class, in these words or similar ones:

    “Until now we have played one note at a time. Today we are going to play three at once, and with them we are going to accompany the oldest song in the world.”

    Write the goal on the board: I can build a chord and say whether it is major or minor.

  2. 5 min

    What they already know: the third and the drone

    Play C and E yourself, then D and F. “What interval is it?” A third. “Are they the same?” No: the first is major and the second, minor. Put on the Seikilos song with the drone and the class remembers the long note in each bar.

  3. 20 min

    New material: the chord, major and minor, the degrees

    1. Take one, skip one (7 min). Play C, E and G yourself, first one by one and then together (Z C B). Write the chord on the board, stacked up. With you, the class builds the chord of D by skipping one: D, F, A. Then each pupil suggests a bottom note and the class builds its chord.
    2. Major and minor (7 min). Play C major and D minor yourself, taking turns. “Day or night? Party or goodbye?” There are no wrong answers: what matters is hearing that they change. Explain that the bottom third decides.
    3. The degrees (6 min). On the Keyboard, press Tonal, KEY C and M. Play Z C B: the chord of I. Change MODE to m: the same keys sound in minor. Change KEY to G: now it is G major.

    With an interactive whiteboard or a projector, the class sees the Seikilos song on the screen with ⛶ FULL; without a screen, copy the chords onto the board.

  4. 17 min

    Application: the class kithara

    Reading the chords (5 min). Project the Seikilos song with chords. Bar by bar, the class reads the bottom note of each chord and says whether it is major or minor: G major, G major, E minor, D minor, G major, F major, G major, D minor, G major. Write the keys for each one on the board.

    Playing with the melody (10 min). First play the chords yourself on the Keyboard while the Seikilos song with the drone is playing: one long chord at the start of each bar. Then the groups take turns: one points to the bar on the screen and the others play the chord, each one playing one of the three notes. The class says whether the chord in each bar is major or minor.

    The sheet (2 min). Each group starts the “Write” part of the sheet; they finish it at home.

  5. 5 min

    Reviewing the goal

    Go back to the sentence on the board. Play four chords at random yourself; the class says “major” or “minor” with their hand up or down.

    “How do you build a chord? What decides whether it is major or minor? Which degrees give major chords?”

    Listen to the answers: take one, skip one; the bottom third; I, IV and V.

Challenge for the quick ones: “Accompany ‘Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star’ with two chords: C major (Z C B) and G major (B M S). Which bars does each one go in?” C at the beginning and at the end; G where the melody “is left hanging in the air”. And one more: “The chord on degree vii of C major is B-D-F. Why is it neither major nor minor?” Because both its thirds are minor: it is diminished.

If they find it hard:
  • They can’t press three keys at once: have three pupils play it, one per key, at the teacher’s signal.
  • They confuse major and minor: play only the two bottom notes (the third) and then the whole chord; have them say “day” or “night”.
  • They get lost in the Seikilos song: slow the demo down with ♩ PULSE and point to each bar yourself.

The Seikilos chords: B M S G major · C B M E minor · X V N D minor · V N A F major. In Tonal, the bottom row is the degrees of the key and Z C B is always the chord of I.

Note names: C = Do · D = Re · E = Mi · F = Fa · G = Sol · A = La · B = Si. Some materials use the syllables: both name the same note.

How to tell it worked: at least three out of four groups play the nine Seikilos chords with the melody; the class gets major or minor right in most bars; and most pupils can build a chord on any note by taking one and skipping one.

Pupil’s sheet · Chords and degrees

To project in class or print. Based on the sheet “Chords and signs” from Elements of Music.

1 · The staircase of the key: the degrees

Each step is a degree, with its Roman numeral. In capitals, the degrees that carry a major chord (I, IV, V); in lower case, those with a minor chord (ii, iii, vi); vii is diminished. Under each step, its key in the Tonal mode of the Cosmic Keyboard.

2 · Major chord and minor chord

The only difference is the middle note: E in the major chord, E♭ in the minor. In the Axiomatic system, E♭ is Shift + C. In the Tonal system you don’t need it: with Mode m the same keys Z C B already play the minor chord.

3 · Inversions

A chord is in inversion when its bottom note is not the root: the same three notes, in a different order.

  • Root position: the root is the lowest note.
  • First inversion: the third is the lowest note.
  • Second inversion: the fifth is the lowest note.

4 · Change the key: the keys follow the tonic

Press the Key buttons: the row of keys does not move, but the note names do. The keyboard button (QWERTY · AZERTY · QWERTZ) changes the letters.

KeyM

In Tonal mode each key of the Cosmic Keyboard shows its degree. When you change Key, the Roman numerals move along the keyboard and the row Z X C V B N M follows them: Z is always the tonic and Z C B always the chord of I. That is why the same key diagram works in any key. In G major, VII falls on a black key: F♯.

Day and night: major and minor

For parents · 10 minutes a week · no music knowledge needed

Today we learned to make chords, three notes at once, and to tell major (bright) from minor (darker). With them we accompanied the oldest song in the world. This week, play together:

  1. Day and night: on the keyboard (musicmotionsuite.com/apps/mmk-demo.html), play the keys Z C B together (C major) and then X V N (D minor). Which one sounds like day and which like night?
  2. The kithara at home: open the Seikilos song with chords in the Score and listen to it. Your child shows you the chords on the lower stave.
  3. Take one, skip one: your child shows you how to build a chord by skipping a note, and you invent one.
  4. The harmony machine: open musicmotionsuite.com/suite/armonia-maquina.html, press PIANO at the top and then SPIN and PLAY. At the top you hear the melody, in the treble clef; at the bottom, the chords, in the bass clef. Which ones sound like day and which like night? No need to sign up.
