In the classroom — How long a sound lasts

How long a sound lasts

Level 1 · Lesson 5 · semibreve, minim and crotchet

Teacher’s guide. © 2026 Music Motion.

For: ages 8 to 12, the whole class. Length: 50 minutes.

Main objective: by the end of the lesson, each pair plays on the keyboard a row of four note values written on the board (crotchets, minims and a semibreve), holding the key down for as many beats as each one lasts; the class counts the metronome clicks and confirms with a thumbs-up.

Secondary objectives: (1) say how many beats each note value lasts: the semibreve four, the minim two and the crotchet one; (2) show with the chocolate bar that a semibreve is worth two minims or four crotchets, and that the crotchet, the quarter, is the pulse.

What you need: a computer with speakers and internet, the board and, if available, a projector. If possible, a real bar of chocolate (or a large drawing of one). It helps to have done Lessons 1 to 4. No music knowledge needed: the keyboard does the work; you guide.

Before the lesson (10 minutes, at home): practise the keyboard. In class your attention needs to be on the children, not on the buttons. Repeat these steps until you can do them without looking at the guide:

  • Open the Cosmic Keyboard and the metronome (pulse, about 60 BPM with tap, start and click, as in Lesson 1).
  • With the metronome running, hold down Z for four clicks, then two, then one: these are the semibreve, the minim and the crotchet. Let go of the key just as the next click arrives.
  • Play the row of the goal: crotchet, crotchet, minim and semibreve, all on Z.
  • Open “Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star” in the Score and press ▶. Notice that each line ends on a minim: “star”, “are”, “high”, “sky”. Before the lesson, leave the two tabs open: the keyboard and the score. In the bar above the score, ⛶ FULL makes it full screen for projecting (Esc to exit) and − / + make the page smaller or larger until the whole piece fits. Remember the ♩ PULSE knob (Lesson 4): it changes the tempo, and the word and the number on the page change with it; a double-click takes it back to the written tempo.

Before we start: the chocolate bar

Three note values

A long or short sound is written with the shape of the note. The semibreve is a whole chocolate bar: it lasts four beats. If we break it in two, each half is a minim: two beats. If we break it into four, each piece is a crotchet: one beat.

The same chocolate bar, broken into 1, 2 and 4 pieces: it is always the same chocolate.

Semibreve (white, with no stem), minim (white, with a stem) and crotchet (filled in, with a stem).

The crotchet is the pulse

The pulse the metronome marks, one click, is how long a crotchet lasts: a quarter of the chocolate bar. This is the most important idea in the lesson, because later on, when they learn about time signatures, the number 4 written at the beginning of the score will come from here: the crotchet is a quarter.

Did you know…? In American English the names already tell the story: the semibreve is the whole note, the minim the half note and the crotchet the quarter note. It is just like the chocolate bar: 1, 1/2 and 1/4.

The lesson, step by step

The lesson follows five stages: the goal, what they already know, the new material, the application and the review of the goal. In each activity, you do it first and the class follows you.

  1. 3 min

    The goal

    Tell the class, in these words or similar ones:

    “We already know where each note lives and what it is called. Today we are going to learn to write how long it lasts. At the end, every pair will play on the keyboard a row of long and short notes written on the board.”

    Write the goal on the board: I can read how long a note lasts and play it.

  2. 5 min

    What they already know: long and short

    With the metronome set slow, play Z short (one click) and then long (four clicks), as in Lesson 2. The class claps once for the short sound and slowly opens their arms while the long one lasts, counting the clicks aloud.

    “In Lesson 2 we drew the long sound with a long line. How do you think we write it on the stave, if the note is always a circle?”

  3. 17 min

    New material: three note values and a chocolate bar

    1. One, two, four (6 min). With the metronome, hold Z down for one click, then two, then four. The class counts aloud with you. Introduce the names and draw each note value on the board as you name it: the one-beat note is the crotchet (filled in, with a stem), the two-beat note the minim (empty, with a stem), the four-beat note the semibreve (empty, with no stem). The pupils copy them onto their sheet.
    2. The chocolate bar (6 min). Show the whole bar: “This is the semibreve, four beats.” Break it in two: “Two minims, two beats each.” Into four: “Four crotchets, one beat each.” Ask first the whole class and then in pairs: “How many crotchets fit into a minim? And into a semibreve?” Finish with the central idea, which the class repeats in their own words: the crotchet is a quarter of the chocolate bar, and it lasts one click: it is the pulse.
    3. Twinkle, Twinkle (5 min). Switch to the score tab and press ▶. The class taps the pulse with a finger on the desk and opens their arms on the long notes. Ask: “Which note value is the long note at the end of each line? How many clicks does it last? How many minims are there in the whole song?” Answer: 6, one at the end of each line.
    4. The tempo game (if there is time left, or to finish). Open ♩ PULSE and turn the knob down until the page says Lento; press ▶. The class taps the pulse with a finger and opens their arms on the minims. Then turn it up to Allegro and do it again. Ask: “Did the song change? And does the minim still last two clicks?” Yes: the speed of the pulse changes, but each note value lasts the same counted in beats. A pupil can choose the tempo and the class guesses the word before looking at it.

    “If I give you a minim of chocolate, how many crotchets am I giving you?”

    With an interactive whiteboard or a projector, the class sees the score on the screen; without a screen, print it with the Score’s PRINT button.

  4. 20 min

    Application: playing how long it lasts

    Write the row of the goal on the board: crotchet, crotchet, minim and semibreve, all on C. Show it yourself first: with the metronome running, play Z, holding the key down for as long as each note value lasts, while you say “ta, ta, ta-a, ta-a-a-a”.

    The row of the goal: crotchet, crotchet, minim and semibreve, all on Z. Underneath, the number of beats each one lasts.

    1. The whole class says the row in syllables (“ta, ta, ta-a, ta-a-a-a”) while you play it again.
    2. A pair comes up to the computer: one points to each note value on the board and the other plays it.
    3. The class counts the clicks aloud and gives a thumbs-up if each note lasted as long as it should.
    4. After two or three pairs, change the row (for example minim, crotchet, crotchet, semibreve). The pairs keep taking turns and swap roles.
    5. While they wait, each pair writes a row of four note values of their own on their sheet, for the review.
  5. 5 min

    Reviewing the goal

    Go back to the sentence on the board. Copy onto the board the row that one of the pairs wrote; the pairs who have not had a turn yet play it, and the class gives a thumbs-up if it matches.

    “How did you know how long to hold the key down? Which note value is the pulse?”

    Listen to the answers: looking at the shape of the note, counting the clicks, and that the crotchet lasts one click.

A challenge for fast finishers: with the score of “Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star” projected, ask: “How many beats does the whole song last?” They need to count the crotchets (1 beat each) and the minims (2 beats each). Answer: 48 (36 crotchets and 6 minims).

With the Melody Machine: how long each note lasts

To close the lesson, or to start the next one. Open the Melody Machine and press SPIN.

  1. Find the note values. On the screen, the class looks for a crotchet (filled-in head with a stem), a minim (empty head with a stem) and, if there is one, a semibreve (an empty head on its own). If they do not appear, spin again.
  2. Count the beats. Press PLAY: the class claps the pulse. On each minim, two claps; on the semibreve, four.
  3. The last bar. The fourth bar usually ends with a long note, “at home”. How many beats does it last?

The machine also writes other note values (with a little dot or with a tail) that they will see in the next lessons.

If they find it hard:
  • They let go of the key too early: count the clicks aloud with them (“one, two, three, four”) and lift your hand at the end, on top of theirs.
  • They mix up the minim and the semibreve: both are empty; the minim has a stem (a little stick) and the semibreve does not. “The semibreve is so long it doesn’t need a stick to hold it up.”
  • They cannot hear the long notes in Twinkle, Twinkle: sing the words while following the score; on “star”, “are”, “high” and “sky” the voice stays for two clicks.
  • The metronome is too fast: slow it down with tap, tapping more slowly. Note values are learned better slowly.

In this lesson one key is enough: Z, the C on the bottom row. What changes is not the note but how long the key is held down.

Note names: C = Do · D = Re · E = Mi · F = Fa · G = Sol · A = La · B = Si. Some materials use the syllables: both name the same note.

How to tell it worked: at least three out of four pairs play the row on the board with the correct length for each note value, and the class confirms it with a thumbs-up. In addition, most pupils say how many beats each note value lasts and explain with the chocolate bar that the crotchet is a quarter.

How long a sound lasts, at home

For parents · 10 minutes a week · no music knowledge needed

Today in class we learned three note values: the semibreve (4 beats), the minim (2) and the crotchet (1). This week, play together:

  1. Open the keyboard on the computer: musicmotionsuite.com/apps/mmk-demo.html
  2. Walking the note values: one person claps a calm pulse and says “crotchet”, “minim” or “semibreve”. The other walks: for the crotchet, one step; for the minim, one step and then wait for one more clap; for the semibreve, one step and then stand still for three more claps. Then swap.
  3. The chocolate bar: break a bar of chocolate (or a sheet of paper) into 1, 2 and 4 pieces. The child says which note value each piece is.
  4. Twinkle, Twinkle: sing “Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star”. The child opens their arms on the long notes at the end of each line.
  5. The Melody Machine: open musicmotionsuite.com/suite/tragamonedas.html, press SPIN and look for a crotchet, a minim and a semibreve. Press PLAY and clap the beats of each one. With PRINT you can print the tune. No registration needed.
